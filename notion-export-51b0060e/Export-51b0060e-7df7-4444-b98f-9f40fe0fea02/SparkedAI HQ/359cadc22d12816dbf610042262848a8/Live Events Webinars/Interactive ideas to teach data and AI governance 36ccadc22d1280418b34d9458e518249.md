# Interactive ideas to teach data and AI governance

**Topics**: For *data* governance: data quality, access, consent, ownership, and lifecycle management. For *AI* governance: model transparency, bias, accountability, monitoring, and risk mitigation. (AI governance sits on top of and  extends the data layer.)

- **Case-based scenarios.** Use realistic dilemmas where learners decide whether a data or AI use case is allowed, what risks exist, and what controls are needed; this is a core approach in learner-centered governance training.
- **Role-playing workshops.** Assign participants roles like data owner, legal, security, product manager, and ethics lead, then have them negotiate a launch decision for an AI feature using shared rules and constraints.
- **Knowledge checks and mini-quizzes.** Short quizzes with immediate feedback help reinforce definitions like ownership, consent, transparency, quality, and security.
- **Data lineage exercises.** Ask learners to trace a dataset from collection to storage to model training to output, then identify where risk, bias, or compliance issues could appear.
- **Governance design sprints.** Small groups create a simple policy, RACI, review checklist, or escalation path for one AI use case, which makes governance feel operational instead of abstract.
- **Red-team / blue-team simulations.** One group tries to find failure modes, privacy leaks, or bias risks; the other group proposes safeguards and monitoring. This works well for AI governance because it highlights both model risk and data controls.

## **Example 60 minute session**

1. **10 minutes:** Introduce a fictional AI hiring or student-support system.
2. **15 minutes:** Small groups map the data flow and identify governance risks.
3. **15 minutes:** Each group decides what controls are needed, such as consent, access limits, human review, or logging.
4. **10 minutes:** Groups present decisions and compare tradeoffs.
5. **10 minutes:** Quick quiz and reflection on what policy changes would reduce risk.
